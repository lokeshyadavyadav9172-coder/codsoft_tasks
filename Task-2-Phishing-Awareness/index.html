01 — THE BASICS

What is phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering technique where attackers impersonate trusted people or organizations to trick victims into revealing information, clicking malicious links, or downloading harmful files.

📧

Phishing Emails

Fake emails designed to make you click a link, open an attachment, or reveal sensitive information.

🔐

Fake Login Pages

Websites that imitate legitimate services to steal usernames, passwords, or verification codes.

🌐

Fraudulent Websites

Fake websites designed to look legitimate while collecting personal or financial information.

02 — SPOT THE TRAPS

Know the warning signs.

Attackers often use predictable techniques. Knowing these signals can help you stop before you click.

01

Urgency & Fear

"Your account will be deleted today!" Attackers create pressure so you act without thinking.

02

Suspicious Sender

Check the complete email address instead of trusting the displayed name.

03

Strange Links

Hover over links and inspect the real destination before opening them.

04

Unexpected Attachments

Be careful with unexpected documents, archives, or executable files.

05

Too Good to Be True

Unexpected prizes, refunds, jobs, or rewards can be used as bait.

06

Requests for Secrets

Legitimate organizations generally should not ask you to reveal passwords or verification codes by email.

03 — SOCIAL ENGINEERING

How attackers manipulate people.

01

Authority

Pretending to be a manager, bank employee, government official, or IT administrator.

02

Urgency

Creating a deadline so the victim acts before verifying the request.

03

Curiosity

Using interesting files, messages, or links to encourage the victim to click.

04

Trust

Impersonating friends, colleagues, brands, or familiar services.

04 — REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES

Learn from actual attacks.

CASE STUDY 01

Fake Microsoft 365 Login

An attacker sends an email claiming that a user's Microsoft account needs verification.

Email → Fake Login → Credentials Stolen
Lesson:

Open the service directly through its official website instead of using unexpected login links.

CASE STUDY 02

Fake Delivery Message

A message claims that a package cannot be delivered and asks the recipient to pay a small fee.

SMS → Payment Link → Data Theft
Lesson:

Verify deliveries through the courier's official website or application.

05 — STAY SAFE

Your anti-phishing playbook.

✓

Use multi-factor authentication on important accounts.

✓

Verify suspicious requests through another trusted channel.

✓

Check the domain name carefully before entering credentials.

✓

Keep your operating system, browser, and security software updated.

✓

Never share passwords or one-time verification codes.

✓

Report suspicious emails instead of simply deleting them.
